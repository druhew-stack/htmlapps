Trust the creators: Each style reflects a different photographer's deliberate choices. Agarwal's Portra is intentionally soft (Sharpness 0). Giosi's Velvia is intentionally sharp (5). Leave the sliders as set unless you have a specific reason to override.

§ 03White Balance & Exposure

Each style has a preferred white balance and exposure compensation. The pattern: Portra wants warmth and slight overexposure; Velvia and B&W want clean daylight and slight underexposure.

Portra 160NC

  • White balanceCloudy
  • Exposure compensation0 to +1/3
  • CharacterSoft, warm, flattering

Velvia by Giosi

  • White balanceDaylight (Auto indoors)
  • Exposure compensation−1/3 to −2/3
  • CharacterSharp, saturated, warm-leaning

TMax

  • White balanceDaylight or Cloudy
  • Exposure compensation−1/3 to −2/3
  • CharacterWarm-toned, nostalgic B&W

Canassic Chrome

  • White balanceDaylight
  • Exposure compensation0 to −1/3
  • CharacterMuted, restrained, documentary

Simplification: If switching WB feels like friction, leave it on Cloudy permanently. Velvia and Canassic Chrome will look slightly warmer than ideal but still good. TMax is nearly unaffected.

§ 05Quick Decision Guide

When you raise the camera, ask: what am I shooting?

People & warm moments → Portra
Vibrant or contrasty outdoor scenes → Velvia by Giosi
Hard shadows or moody atmosphere → TMax
Fluorescent or messy light → TMax
Street / documentary / restrained editorial → Canassic Chrome
Overcast urban scenes → Canassic Chrome or TMax
Bright sunny landscape → Velvia by Giosi
Indoor with people → Portra or Velvia
When in doubt → Portra

Memory aid: Portra wants + exposure (lift highlights). Velvia, TMax, and Canassic Chrome want − exposure (deepen colour and shadow). Canassic Chrome lives in Faithful with Dave Lawrence's tuning — not a User Def. slot.

§ 06Technique Notes

For nostalgic B&W feel

  • Shoot at ISO 1600 – 3200 for film-like grain.
  • Underexpose 1/3 to 2/3 stop for dense shadows.
  • Look for directional light — strong shadows are graphic elements.
  • Use Live View when composing for B&W (optical viewfinder shows colour).
  • TMax's subtle warm tone is built in by JLN — no filter adjustment needed.

For the 24mm pancake (38mm equiv)

  • Get closer than feels natural — proximity is the focal length's strength.
  • Watch the corners of the frame, not just the subject.
  • Foreground · middle ground · background creates depth.
  • Close-focus to ~16cm for near-macro detail work.

Workflow safety net

  • RAW files are unaffected by Picture Style — you keep full flexibility.
  • JPEG gives instant shareable results; RAW saves your keepers.
  • For tricky light, just shoot — decide aesthetic in DPP later.
