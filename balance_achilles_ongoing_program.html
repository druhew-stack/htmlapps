Achilles — the heavy session (3×/week)
Two movements now, not one: the standing straight-leg raise (gastrocnemius) and the seated bent-knee raise (soleus). Both loaded with a dumbbell — easier to balance and to load each leg independently than a backpack. Both left-first. The soleus is now a core movement — it's the main running muscle and a key protector of the Achilles.
1
Standing dumbbell calf raise off step — LEFT first
Gastrocnemius focus. Single leg on the step, leg straight, heel drops below toe level for full range. Dumbbell in the hand on the working-leg side, other hand on a wall/rail for balance. Light assist only on reps where the muscle genuinely fatigues.
3 sets × 6–8 reps3s up / 3s down
2
Standing dumbbell calf raise off step — RIGHT
Same protocol. Load each leg to its own effort — the dumbbell lets you run the right heavier than the left if it's ready, rather than a backpack forcing one shared weight.
3 sets × 6–8 reps
3
Seated dumbbell soleus raise — LEFT first
Sit on a chair/bench, ball of foot on the step, heel free to drop. Rest the dumbbell vertically on your thigh just above the knee. The bent knee is built into the position and isolates the soleus — no balancing act. Drive up slowly through the ball of the foot.
3 sets × 8–12 reps3s up / 3s down
4
Seated dumbbell soleus raise — RIGHT
Same protocol, load to its own effort. Expect the soleus to start lighter than the standing raise — that's leverage, not weakness. Let the rep range and effort be the constant, not the weight.
3 sets × 8–12 reps
Warm up first, every time: heavy load on a cold, stiff tendon is the main way to aggravate it. Before the dumbbell: a short walk or a minute of marching, ankle circles, then a few light bodyweight raises to prime the tendon. This matters most on Monday — it's a rest day so you start cold. Wed/Fri you may already be warm from your strength circuit, but still do a couple of priming raises before the heavy sets. Cool-down stretch is optional now — the full-range loaded raises already move the tendon through its range, so a post-session stretch (straight-leg + bent-knee, 30s each) is for ankle mobility, not the tendon. Keep it gentle, never force end-range, and skip it if you feel any heel-bone pinch.
Order within the session: standing raises first while you're freshest, seated soleus second. Always left before right within each movement. Total session is still short — roughly 12–15 min. Note: the standing raise still trains single-leg balance; the seated raise doesn't, but you get balance work elsewhere.
Phase progression (criteria, not a calendar)
Tendon work advances on readiness, not dates — you move to the next phase when you meet the criteria, whether that takes 2 weeks or 5. Timeframes below are rough guides only.
1
Consolidate ← YOU ARE HERE
Goal: all 3 sets clean and unassisted on the LEFT, both movements, at your current dumbbell weight. Right is basically there; left needs set 3 to come good. Advance when: 3 clean sets each side, both movements, no next-day flare. ~2–4 weeks.
2
Build load
Goal: progressively grab a heavier dumbbell, keeping straight-leg at 6–8 reps and soleus at 8–12. Add a little only when the current load is clean. If grip becomes the limiter on the standing raise before the calf does, switch that movement to a loaded pack or weight vest. Advance when: you can load meaningfully heavier than bodyweight with full range and good control. ~6–12 weeks, ongoing.
3
Energy-storage / running prep
Goal: layer in hops, skips and faster tempo work so the tendon tolerates the spring demands of running. Soleus capacity is what this phase leans on most. Advance when: heavy work is solid, pain-free, and you're ramping run volume toward the triathlon. When heavy phase is established.
4
Maintain
Goal: hold the gains with heavy calf work (both movements) 1–2×/week indefinitely, alongside your run/ride load. Ongoing.
Step / range note: the off-step (full-range) version is the harder, better progression — not a mistake. But if you feel pinching right at the heel bone at the very bottom of the drop, that's a compression signal — limit to flat-ground range on that side. Mid-tendon loading that settles within 24h is fine at full depth.

You hit Left 19s / Right 25s — both clear your age markers (norm ~13s, under-40 ~15s). You've earned the drop from the full daily routine. This is now about holding the gains and closing the 6s left–right gap.

Habit-stack option
If a structured session slips, fold it into daily life: eyes-closed single-leg while brushing teeth, or single-leg balance during your strength-day rest periods. Counts toward maintenance.
opportunistic
No tendon warm-up needed here: the warm-up is only to protect a cold tendon under heavy load — SLEC just holds bodyweight, which the tendon handles all day. Step straight in. The only prep worth a few seconds is for balance, not the tendon: a couple of gentle weight shifts to feel your foot's contact and sharpen the proprioceptive input.
Frequency: 2–3 short sessions a week holds these numbers — slotted onto your Tue/Thu cycling days above, with an optional Sunday third. Re-benchmark both legs every 6–8 weeks; you want the left drifting up toward the right. Once they're within ~3s, you can stop thinking about asymmetry and just maintain.
The rules that keep this safe
Same principles you've been using — collected in one place.
24h
The 24-hour rule
Ache during and after heavy loading is fine if it settles within 24h and the tendon isn't stiffer the next morning. Sharp pain, or worse-next-day stiffness, means you progressed too fast — drop back one rung.
L
Left first, always
The left lags on both balance (19 vs 25) and strength (set 3). Train it first when you're freshest, give it the extra set, and let the right match the left's volume rather than racing ahead.
C
Collagen timing — unchanged
15g hydrolysed collagen + vitamin C, 30–60 min before the heavy Achilles session. One morning dose primes the system for the day; no need to double up.
6–8w
Re-benchmark cycle
Every 6–8 weeks: re-test SLEC both legs, and check whether all 3 sets are clean and unassisted on the left for both the straight-leg and bent-knee (soleus) raises. Those readouts tell you when to progress a phase or hold.
∞
It doesn't fully stop
Tendon rebuild is a 3–6 month job, then ongoing maintenance (heavy calf 1–2×/week) indefinitely — especially with 100km rides and a triathlon ahead. Balance is the same: use it or lose it.
Decision shortcut: SLEC → maintain (you passed). Achilles → keep progressing (structural rebuild takes months, you're ~1 month in). The left side is the thread tying both together — close that gap and everything else follows.
