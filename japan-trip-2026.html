Christmas Day — Kiyosumi-Shirakawa & Monzen-Nakacho + KFC 🍗
  • Morning: take the metro east to Kiyosumi-Shirakawa (~15 min) — one of Tokyo's most quietly stylish neighbourhoods. Third-wave coffee shops, art galleries, and old wooden houses along the canals.
  • Kiyosumi Garden: one of Tokyo's loveliest traditional gardens, much less crowded than Hamarikyu. Stepping stones across the pond, koi, and tea house views.
  • ☕ Coffee stop at Blue Bottle Kiyosumi — the original Blue Bottle in Japan, set in a converted warehouse. Worth the pilgrimage.
  • 🍱 Lunch: walk south to Monzen-Nakacho — a proper old-Tokyo shitamachi neighbourhood famous for its izakayas. Try a local set lunch or grab tendon (tempura rice bowls) from one of the neighbourhood spots.
  • Tomioka Hachimangu Shrine: atmospheric and historic, home to one of Tokyo's three great festivals. Quiet and uncrowded on Christmas Day.
  • 🍗 Christmas Dinner: KFC! 🎄 Pre-order a Christmas party barrel via the KFC Japan website (sells out — book by mid-December). Pick up at a local branch. 'Kurisumasu ni wa Kentakkii' — genuinely festive and very Japanese.

Mt Fuji Views — Kawaguchiko
  • Highway bus from Shinjuku to Kawaguchiko Lake (~1h 45m) — book in advance, runs regularly
  • Lake Kawaguchi offers one of Japan's most iconic winter views: snow-capped Fuji reflecting in still water. December is one of the best months for visibility.
  • Kachi Kachi Ropeway: short cable car up the mountain for elevated Fuji panoramas — great for the boys
  • 🍲 Lunch: Houtou Fudo — famous for hōtō, a flat-noodle miso hot-pot that's the regional specialty. Hearty, warming, and perfect for a cold mountain day.
  • Chureito Pagoda is an easy side trip for the classic five-storey pagoda + Fuji shot (20 min from Kawaguchiko by train)

OTHER NEIGHBOURHOODS WORTH AN AFTERNOON
Kuramae 🪡
Literally next door to you in Taito City — 'Tokyo's Brooklyn'. Artisan shops (leather, paper, pens), small-batch coffee (Leaves Coffee, Dandelion Chocolate), independent designers. Easy slow morning right from home.
Shibamata
30 min east on the Keisei line — feels like time-travel to 1950s Tokyo. Taishakuten Temple, an old shotengai full of sweet shops, and the Tora-san Museum (most beloved Japanese film series). Atmospheric and very local.
Tsukishima & Tsukuda
Old fishing-town pocket in central Tokyo. Tsukishima is home to monjayaki (Tokyo's pancake-batter griddle dish, kids love cooking it themselves). Walk the Sumida canals afterwards.
Kagurazaka
French-Japanese neighbourhood with hidden alley yokocho. Atmospheric backstreets, great cafes, and surprisingly few tourists. Best in the late afternoon.
Tomigaya / Okusawa
Quiet residential pockets near Yoyogi Park / Nakameguro. Excellent cafes, indie bookshops, and slow Tokyo at its best. Pair with a walk along the Meguro River.
Akihabara
15 min walk or one stop from Ueno — for a few hours if the boys are into gaming, electronics, or anime. Save the full visit for Oscar's birthday day in January.
Mt Fuji visibility
December is one of the best months for Fuji views — clear, cold air and a snow cap. Check the weather the night before; a cloudy day at Kawaguchiko is worth postponing for.
Cup Noodles Museum
Book online in advance — this fills up fast, especially in school holidays. The custom cup noodle experience takes about 45 min and the boys will love it.

🎒
Why Shimokitazawa is perfect for slow travel: a bohemian, low-rise neighbourhood that locals adore. It's car-free in places, the streets are narrow and pedestrian, and you can spend a whole afternoon just wandering. Direct trains to Shibuya (5 min) and Kichijoji (15 min) make day trips easy.
GETTING THERE FROM MADARAO
Step 1
Taxi or shuttle from Madarao to Iiyama Station (~35 min). Pre-book to be sure.
Step 2
Hokuriku Shinkansen: Iiyama → Tokyo Station (~100 min). Book reserved seats in advance.
Step 3
Tokyo Station → Shimokitazawa: JR Chuo Line to Shinjuku, then Odakyu line (~25 min, ~¥300). Or just take a taxi from Shinjuku (~15 min).
Total time
~3 to 3.5 hours door-to-door. Leaving Madarao around 12pm gets you settled in Shimokitazawa by 4pm.

Inokashira Park & Kichijoji
  • Take the Keio Inokashira line west from Shimokitazawa to Kichijoji (~15 min, direct). One of Tokyo's most livable neighbourhoods.
  • Inokashira Park: a big lake with paddle boats (kids will love), walking trails, street performers on weekends. Easy, slow, beautiful.

  • Afternoon option 1: Ghibli Museum (Mitaka) — needs booking in advance (tickets release the 1st of each month, sell out fast). If you can get them, this is unmissable.
  • Afternoon option 2: Kichijoji shopping streets — small boutiques, vintage, kids' shops, anime stores
  • 🍣 Dinner: back in Shimokitazawa — try Shirohige's Cream Puff Factory for dessert (Totoro-shaped cream puffs!), or a casual sushi spot

Meiji Shrine, Yoyogi, Harajuku → Aoyama

SHIMOKITAZAWA NEIGHBOURHOOD NOTES
Vintage shopping

Coffee scene
Bear Pond Espresso (small, intense), Cafe Stay Happy (cozy), and several small roasters. The whole area is a coffee lover's paradise.
Curry

Two relatively new developments around the station with small indie shops, cafes, and food — clean modern Tokyo right next to the old alleys.
Walking, not trains

Yokohama option

THE PLAN — JAN 24
AM
Pokemon Center Mega Tokyo — Ikebukuro
  • Japan's largest Pokemon Center, on the 2nd floor of Sunshine City. Aim to be there at opening (10:00) before the crowds.
  • Let Oscar pick out a birthday Pokemon — plush, trading card pack, or one of the exclusive Tokyo-only items he won't find anywhere else.
  • Pokemon Cafe is in the same complex but needs a lottery booking ~31 days in advance. Worth trying — if you win the lottery, build lunch around it.
LUNCH
🍣 Sushi Train — Genki Sushi or Uobei (Shibuya)
  • Hop the JR Yamanote line from Ikebukuro to Shibuya (~15 min).
  • Genki Sushi or Uobei Shibuya — touch-screen ordering, sushi delivered on a high-speed track straight to your seat. Pure spectacle for an 11-year-old.
  • Backup option: any Sushiro or Kura Sushi branch — same concept, equally fun.
PM
Akihabara — Arcades, Gaming & Anime
  • Train from Shibuya to Akihabara (~25 min via Yamanote). Tokyo's gaming, anime, and electronics district.
  • Super Potato: legendary 3-floor retro-gaming store. Old Nintendo, Sega, Game Boy carts everywhere — incredible for an 11-year-old who's curious about gaming history.
  • Hey (Hirose Entertainment Yard) and Taito Station: multi-floor arcades with claw machines (UFO catchers), rhythm games, and racing sims. Bring a stack of 100-yen coins.
  • Nintendo Tokyo (if heading toward Shibuya Parco instead): official Nintendo flagship store with exclusive merch and a Mario photo op. Either Akihabara OR Nintendo Tokyo — both is a lot in one day.
  • Don Quijote Akihabara: massive discount store with weird and wonderful Japan-only finds. Good place for souvenir shopping.
DINNER
🥩 Birthday Yakiniku + Cake
  • Yakiniku (Japanese BBQ) is perfect for a kid's birthday — everyone grills their own at the table, very interactive.
  • Gyu-Kaku is a reliable casual chain with branches everywhere — great for families, English menus, kid-friendly.
  • For something more special: Han no Daidokoro or Yakiniku Toraji — higher-end wagyu, still kid-friendly, worth the upgrade for a birthday.
  • 🍰 Cake: pick up earlier in the day from any depachika — Isetan Shinjuku, Mitsukoshi Ginza, or Takashimaya all have astonishing patisserie sections. Bring it to dinner or save for the hotel room.
  • Most yakiniku places will let you bring a small cake if you ask politely — call ahead.
EVE
Evening — Karaoke or Quiet Wind-Down
  • If everyone still has energy: an hour at Karaoke Kan or Big Echo. Family rooms, all-you-can-drink soft drinks, and Oscar can pick whatever songs he wants. A very Japanese birthday memory.
  • Otherwise: head back to the hotel for cake, presents, and rest before the flight tomorrow.
SWAPS IF OSCAR PREFERS OTHER THINGS
If he loves Ghibli
Try to get Ghibli Museum tickets the day they release (1st of each month, sells out in minutes). If not, Donguri Republic stores in Tokyo Station Ichibangai are the next best thing.

If the weather is rotten

Tokyo → Madarao (Dec 28)
Hokuriku Shinkansen from Ueno Station to Iiyama (~95 min). Look for E7/W7 series trains. Pre-book reserved seats. Then taxi or pre-arranged shuttle to Madarao (~35 min, ~¥4,000).
IC Card (Suica/Pasmo)
Load one each for Tokyo — works on all trains, buses, and konbini. Essential for smooth city travel with kids.
Madarao Driving
You'll have a car at Madarao — this opens up everything: Suginohara, Myoko, Snow Monkeys, Matsumoto, Togakushi. Worth planning day trips by group around weather/snow conditions.
Guest Families
Hokuriku Shinkansen to Iiyama is the easiest route for all three families arriving from Tokyo. Share the taxi/shuttle info with each in advance.
Ski Rentals
Pre-booking ski and snowboard rentals is recommended, especially for beginner families. Madarao resort base has rentals but popular sizes book out.
SIM / Pocket WiFi
A Japanese SIM or pocket WiFi is essential. Get this at the airport on arrival — Haneda has several counters. IIJmio or Docomo tourist SIMs are reliable.
Money
Japan is still quite cash-based in rural areas. ATMs at 7-Eleven and Japan Post reliably accept foreign cards. Get yen before leaving the city.
Oliver's Birthday
Dec 30 — consider booking a restaurant in Iiyama in advance. Japanese New Year period can get busy. Alternatively, a home fondue / nabe night is very cosy.
